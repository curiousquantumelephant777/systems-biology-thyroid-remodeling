## Functional Med Insight: Targeted Genomic Testing

I recently did a Tempus genomic test which validated a genetic hypothesis I had. This confirmed I am homozygous for the *MTHFR C677T* variant. This specific mutation reduces the rate of enzymatic conversion of 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate to 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF/L-methylfolate) by approximately 70% [[1]](#ref1). This assay was performed using genomic DNA isolated from saliva via buccal swabs for analysis by a combination of matrix-assisted laser desorption ionization, time of flight (MALDI-TOF) mass spectrometry, and capillary electrophoresis for genotyping [[1]](#ref1).

5-MTHF acts as an important methyl donor for remethylating homocysteine into methionine. This generates S-adenosylmethionine (SAMe), the principal methyl donor required for the synthesis of common monoamine neurotransmitters (e.g., serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine). This creates a metabolic bottleneck that impacts mood and neuropsychiatric function. This illustrates how metabolic factors play a significant role in mental health. Interestingly, I was doing research a few months ago and found this variant is linked significantly to autistic phenotypes [[2]](#ref2).

Concurrently, I discovered from this assay that I have the heterozygous (Val66Met/rs6265) variant for Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF), a protein involved in neural plasticity and learning. One Met allele is associated with slightly altered activity-dependent BDNF secretion. Specifically, it impairs the intracellular trafficking and activity-dependent secretion of BDNF, while leaving constitutive secretion unaffected [[3]](#ref3). This aligns with my hypothesis that I have impaired BDNF signaling.

### Functional Med Interventions

MTHFR Variant Remethylation:

- **Methylated folate (5-MTHF)**: Bypasses the MTHFR metabolic bottleneck by providing the active 5-MTHF required to convert homocysteine to methionine, restoring SAMe pool availability for monoamine neurotransmitter synthesis (serotonin, dopamine, norepinephrine).
-  **Beets**: Beets contain high amounts of betaine (trimethylglycine), which helps lower homocysteine levels.

BDNF/Neuroplasticity Stimulation:

* **Long-distance running:** Robustly upregulates central BDNF transcription via PGC-1α/FNDC5 signaling.
* **Zinc:** Stimulates BDNF expression by modulating NMDA receptor tone and promoting TrkB transactivation.

### Implications and Future Directions
This experience underscores the importance of systems biology in medicine. This assay should be considered when doing a neuropsychiatric assessment to allow for more tailored and integrative treatment. 

### Disclaimer
According to Tempus, these results are classified as Emerging Evidence Results and have not yet met the threshold to be clinically actionable [[1]](#ref1).
